Net Thermal Capacity Trends

What happens to each state's dispatchable thermal fleet when EIA-860M scheduled retirements (coal, gas, oil, nuclear) are netted against the gas GEM shows in development. Through 2040 the U.S. adds far more firm capacity than it retires — but the gains pile up in gas-building red states while several blue states run net-negative.

Net thermal capacity change by state

Red = net additions (new gas outweighs retirements); blue = net losses (retirements outweigh additions). Here red and blue mark net gain vs. loss, not the political split. Hover or click a state for detail.

Gas vs. Wind and Solar

Who builds more by 2040?

Capacity added through 2040: the GEM U.S. natural-gas buildout — pipeline (370 GW) and pipeline + utility IRP (451 GW) — vs. AEO2026 expected wind & solar capacity growth.

How much power do they actually deliver?

Annual electricity generated: the GEM gas buildout (370 GW pipeline and 451 GW with IRP) at three illustrative capacity factors vs. EIA's AEO2026 expected wind & solar generation growth, 2025–2040.

How does it compare to EIA's 2022 forecast?

Cumulative U.S. gas capacity added, at five-year milestones: GEM's proposed plants placed by their announced online year (all remaining undated GEM capacity shown in 2050) vs. the additions EIA projected in its 2022 Annual Energy Outlook — a forecast made before the data-center demand surge. GEM = nameplate; AEO2022 = net summer capacity (~10–15% lower), so the AEO bars if anything understate the gap. By 2030, GEM already shows ~270 GW to EIA's 148 GW.
